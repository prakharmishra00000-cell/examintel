   466→17. QUESTION EVOLUTION LEVELS
   467→For every source question, generate up to five evolution levels.
   468→LEVEL 1 — SAME CONCEPT, EASIER
   469→Keep the underlying concept the same but simplify:
   470→Numbers
   471→Language
   472→Reasoning complexity
   473→Calculation
   474→Number of steps
   475→Purpose:
   476→Build conceptual confidence.
   477→
   478→LEVEL 2 — SAME CONCEPT, DIFFERENT FRAMING
   479→Keep the same core concept but change:
   480→Scenario
   481→Wording
   482→Question structure
   483→Context
   484→Data presentation
   485→Purpose:
   486→Ensure the student understands the concept rather than memorizing the original question.
   487→
   488→LEVEL 3 — MULTI-CONCEPT
   489→Combine the original concept with another related concept.
   490→Example:
   491→Percentage + Ratio
   492→or:
   493→Integration + Differential Equations
   494→Purpose:
   495→Test concept integration.
   496→
   497→LEVEL 4 — DIFFICULT VARIANT
   498→Increase:
   499→Complexity
   500→Number of steps
   501→Distractor quality
   502→Calculation difficulty
   503→Conceptual depth
   504→Purpose:
   505→Prepare students for difficult questions.
   506→
   507→LEVEL 5 — EXAM-TRAP VARIANT
   508→Create a legitimate difficult question designed around common misconceptions.
   509→Examples:
   510→Similar-looking formulas
   511→Unit traps
   512→Sign errors
   513→Logical traps
   514→Close answer choices
   515→Misleading wording
   516→Do not create ambiguous or invalid questions merely to make them difficult.
